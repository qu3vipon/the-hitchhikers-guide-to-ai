# For hitchhikers

We live in a time when a few words can prompt AI to write code and even deploy a service. You might wonder whether it still makes sense to learn by understanding concepts one at a time and implementing things yourself.

Teaching AI and programming to people from many different backgrounds has taught me something: the people who use tools well are those who can ask their own questions and judge the results. That is why I believe **the ability to think for yourself** is essential to using AI effectively. AI can quickly produce a draft, offer a hint when you are stuck, and suggest several approaches. But it cannot take over the responsibility of deciding what to build, judging whether the result is what you wanted, or finding the next question when something goes wrong.

This guide is also being created with AI. I use it to help research material and refine the writing. But I decide what is worth learning and why, how the topics should connect, and what learners really need. We can use AI as a tool while still developing **our own clear preferences and standards** for what we create and the choices we make.

Learning to produce results quickly matters. There are also people who still want to **think things through, understand, and discover the answers for themselves**. I made this guide for those people: hitchhikers trying to find their way through the vast, unfamiliar world of the AI age.

I want to offer learning material that you can work through independently, one step at a time. I hope this guide helps you find your next question and build your own answers along the way.
